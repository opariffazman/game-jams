01 / Research

What the loop research says

Core loop first

A core loop is the cycle the player repeats most. Test it: can you state it in one sentence? If not, cut scope.

Hyper-casual games use one tap or swipe. They struggle with long retention, so the fix is a thin layer on top: collection, daily goals, or a second mode. The industry calls this hybrid-casual.

Sources: Unity glossary, Homa Academy, Udonis on hybrid-casual.

Three loops, stacked

  • Seconds: act, feel, reward. Needs juice: sound, particles, haptics.
  • Minutes: run ends, instant retry in under one second, best score.
  • Days: daily seed, unlock a palette, share a result.

A jam game needs the first two loops done well. The third loop costs one day if the design allows it. Proposals below say which they support.

Accessibility baseline

  • Every essential action works with one input. Xbox Accessibility Guidelines (XAG 108) ask for this.
  • Color never carries meaning alone. Add shape, pattern, or text.
  • Offer reduced motion and no flashes. Offer hold-or-toggle.
  • Offer a no-timer or slow mode.
  • Mirror the layout for left or right thumb.

Sources: Games for Change tips (PDF), Game accessibility.

02 / Shared chassis

Rules every proposal obeys

Input
Tap, hold, or swipe anywhere in the bottom zone. A second layout mirrors it for the other thumb. Keyboard maps for desktop on itch.io, including the Shift keys, as an easter egg.
Screen
Base 540x960, stretch mode canvas_items, aspect expand. HUD respects safe area. Test on real iOS Safari and Android Chrome early.
First tap
A "Tap to start" screen unlocks browser audio and enters fullscreen.
Assist
Slow mode, no-timer mode, reduced motion, no-flash, high contrast, and larger text. All toggles live on the title screen and in pause.
Feedback
Every event gets sound, a shape change, and optional haptics. Input.vibrate_handheld works on Android. iOS Safari has no vibration API, so haptics stay optional.
Export
Single-thread web export runs on itch.io with no extra headers. Tick "Mobile friendly" on the itch page. Glow support in Compatibility depends on the Godot version, so fake bloom with additive sprites until confirmed.

Godot web options (canvas resize policy, PWA orientation, threads): EditorExportPlatformWeb docs. The PWA orientation option only applies when the game installs as an app, not inside the itch.io frame.

03 / Proposals

Five games

P1 / reflex puzzle

Bitshift

A byte of lit bits falls toward a gate. Shift the whole byte left or right so lit bits meet the holes. A bit pushed off the edge is gone for good. That overflow is the cost and the trick.

Byte falls→Shift << or >>→Bits hit holes→Pulse + combo→Faster→Retry < 1 s
Thumb
Left half of screen is <<. Right half is >>. Swipe works as an alias. One thumb covers both halves.
Style
Black screen, one acid accent, chunky mono type. Fake CRT bloom, scanline wipe on every pass, kick-drum tied to the combo.
Loops
Seconds: shift and pulse. Minutes: 60 s runs with instant retry. Days: daily seed plus palette unlocks.
Access
Bits show position numbers and holes use pattern, not only color. Slow mode cuts fall speed. Calm mode becomes turn-based (see proposal P4).
Godot
Simple 2D. Rows are ColorRect nodes on a fixed tick. A canvas shader adds scanlines. Low risk.
Risk
The game must teach binary without text. Level one uses a single bit and a single hole.

P2 / rhythm runner

World Flip

An endless vertical run through two overlapping realities. Circles block world A. Squares block world B. One tap swaps worlds. Tap on the beat to stay in sync.

Obstacle ahead→Tap to flip→Pass on beat→Streak→Crash→Retry < 1 s
Thumb
Tap anywhere. Hold is a second input only in later stages, with a tap-toggle alternative.
Style
Hard-cut duotone. Black and acid swap on every flip. Track music is the level. Obstacles snap to the beat.
Loops
Seconds: flip. Minutes: three tracks, best streak each. Days: daily track, ghost of a friend is not needed.
Access
Shape tells the world, not color. The full-screen swap is a wipe, not a flash, and a reduced-motion toggle removes it. Audio pitch also tells the world. Slow mode halves tempo.
Godot
Two node groups toggle collision and visibility. A screen-texture shader inverts colors. Safest build. Music sync needs a tuned latency offset for web audio.
Risk
Familiar genre. Needs a standout twist. Photosensitivity is the main accessibility risk.

P3 / microgame rush

Shift Change

You work the night shift at a convenience store. Every three seconds the task shifts: bag the item, scare the raccoon, swipe the card. Customers get stranger as the shift goes on.

Prompt→One gesture→Win or lose a life→Speed up→Boss customer→Clock out
Thumb
Tap, hold, or swipe in the bottom zone. Each microgame uses exactly one gesture.
Style
Flat poster vector, thick outlines, three colors per scene, comic sound effects. The store gets darker as the night passes.
Loops
Seconds: one gesture. Minutes: one shift of about two minutes. Days: new customer unlocks in a shift diary.
Access
The prompt word and an icon both show the gesture. A timer-off mode gives unlimited time per task. Speed ramp has a cap.
Godot
One scene per microgame, driven by a shared Resource. Simple code, many assets.
Risk
Content-heavy. A solo jam needs about 12 microgames to feel alive. Highest content cost here.

P4 / daily swipe puzzle

Wraparound

A grid of tiles forms a hidden picture once sorted. Swipe a row or column. Tiles that leave one edge wrap to the other. Rebuild the picture inside a move budget.

See target→Swipe a line→Tiles wrap→Picture forms→Share result→Tomorrow's grid
Thumb
Drag on a row or column. Tap-select plus two shift buttons is the no-drag alternative.
Style
Generative art. Each solved day adds a tile to a growing gallery wall. Soft motion, paper grain, calm audio.
Loops
Seconds: swipe and click-snap. Minutes: one puzzle. Days: daily seed and an emoji grid to copy and share.
Access
Best of the five. Turn-based, no timer, undo is free. Tiles use shape plus color. Fully playable with a switch that moves a cursor.
Godot
Grid in Control nodes. Daily seed from the date hash. Clipboard copy needs a tap and works on web.
Risk
Lowest hype factor. Generating fair puzzles needs a solver or a scramble-from-solved method. Scramble-from-solved is easy.

P5 / perspective puzzler

Tilt Room

A tiny isometric room. Turn it with a thumb dial. When two edges line up from your view, a gap closes and the walker crosses. Walls become floors. Think Monument Valley, scoped to a jam.

Read the room→Turn the dial→Edges align→Walker crosses→Next room
Thumb
One dial at the bottom. Snap to 90 degree steps with a haptic click. Walker moves on its own.
Style
The most stylish pitch. Pastel isometric solids, long soft shadows, slow ambient music. Screenshots sell it.
Loops
Seconds: turn and align. Minutes: one room. Days: none. Rooms do not replay, so content equals playtime.
Access
No timer, no reflex, easy for one hand. Color-only hints are banned. Rotation speed has a reduce-motion setting.
Godot
Orthographic Camera3D with snap steps, or a fake 2D rotation with pre-rendered sprites. 3D on web Compatibility works but costs more.
Risk
Highest. Each room needs careful level design, and 8 to 10 rooms is the minimum. Weak replay loop.

05 / Recommendation

Build Bitshift with a Wraparound calm mode

Both games share one mechanic: shift a row of bits and lose what falls off the edge. Bitshift is the stylish, fast mode. Wraparound is the turn-based mode with no timer and the daily puzzle. One mechanic gives two audiences and covers the accessibility story.

  • Week 1: Bitshift core, one level, tap halves, instant retry, bloom and sound.
  • Week 2: Level set, daily seed, assist toggles, mirrored layout.
  • Week 3: Calm mode from the same shift code. Palette unlocks.
  • Week 4: Real-phone testing on iOS Safari and Android Chrome, itch.io page, trailer GIF, polish.

World Flip is the fallback if Bitshift does not feel good by the end of week one. Its build risk is lowest.
