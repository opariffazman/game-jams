Game Off 2026 / SHIFT / Hot Swap / direction proposal

Clock in.
Climb. Clock out.

Decision: lock the overall shape of Hot Swap. Each night shift is one run up a tower, built from generated rooms that get harder floor by floor. Lose all hearts and the shift ends. You start again at the bottom and keep what you earned. Hades is the model for the loop. The swap puzzle is the combat.

Run: about 10 minutes Fail = lose a heart, not the run 0 hearts = back to the lobby Meta upgrades persist Turn-based, no timer Replay reel per run

01 / The run in one picture

A tower. Two doors per room.

Rooms
12 per run: 4 floors of 3 rooms, then a boss. Each room is a Hot Swap puzzle generated from the run seed, floor, and heat.
Doors
After each room, pick one of two doors. The door shows its reward: a boon, a heart, tips, or an elite room that pays more and is harder.
Hearts
Start with 3. Fail a room by getting shot or by running out of swaps and you lose one heart and retry that room. Zero hearts ends the run.
Death
You wake in the lobby. You keep tips and unlocks, lose boons and hearts, and the tower is a new seed. A death reel replays your last swaps in 3D.
Win
Beat the boss and get a run recap reel with the best chain kills, a share code, and a new heat tier.

03 / The problem we measured

Random tapping wins about three rooms in four

I ran 300 random-tap games on each of the 63 current rooms. Win rate was 67 to 79 percent in every tier. Tier 5 was no harder than tier 1. Par was 2 swaps in 40 of the 48 generated rooms in tiers 1 to 4.

Cause: unlimited swaps, free retries, one gunman type, uniform random placement, and a difficulty score that counts gunmen instead of decisions.

What changes

  • Swap budget: par plus a small slack that shrinks by floor. Overrunning it fails the room.
  • Hearts: a failed room costs a heart, so trial and error has a price.
  • Metrics: the generator rejects a room when random tapping wins too often.
  • Variety: more gunman types and turn events, shown next.

Greedy trap: the tap that kills the most right now is not optimal. Best solutions: how many winning sequences exist at the minimum swaps. Method after Beukman et al. (search effort) and Shen and Sturtevant (minimum solution length).

05 / Variety kit

Every room asks a different question

Rule: each new piece forces a different approach, and everything is shown at the start of the turn. A room is a puzzle, not a dice roll, which is the model that Into the Breach uses. Ten gunman types make 45 pairs and 420 groups of four, so mixing multiplies variety without new art.

Gunman types

F2Shield

Blocks shots from the front. Forces you to flank or use friendly fire from the side.

F3Knife

No gun. Walks one cell toward you each turn, so waiting costs you. Forces speed.

F3Barrel

A prop that explodes when shot or hit. Chains kills. Forces you to line up blasts.

F4Shotgun

Fires a wide cone. Hits whole groups, so swapping puts a friend in a big area.

Turn events, when a room is not cleared in one turn

F4Reinforcement

A door marks a spot. One turn later a new gunman appears there. Plan for it now.

F3Advance

Survivors step one cell toward you with arrows shown. The room reshapes every turn.

F2Jam

One marked gunman cannot fire next turn. A free kill for the right swap.

F4Ricochet

Bullets bounce once off the walls. Lines now cross in new places.

All events are deterministic from the seed, so the solver includes them when it checks a room and sets par.

Room templates and a novelty filter

Rooms come from motifs, like Spelunky's templates: Crossfire, Bodyguard, Pincer, Shield Wall, Corridor, Barrel Row. Each motif is mirrored, shifted, and given random cover, then passed through the solver gates. A new gunman type appears first in an easy room, then in combination later in the floor.

Novelty filter: never offer the same motif twice in a row, and reject a room whose gunman mix and solution shape match either of the last two rooms.

06 / Between runs

The locker and heat

Locker upgrades (spend tips)

  • Max hearts: 3 to 5 in two steps.
  • Spare slack: +1 swap in the first two floors.
  • Opening choice: pick one boon from three at the start of a run.
  • Intel: unlock new boons in the pool, one at a time.
  • Style: palettes and silhouette outfits for reels.

Upgrades widen options and give a safety net. They never replace skill, because the gates still apply.

Heat (opt in, more reward)

  • Tight Budget: -1 swap slack in every room.
  • Early Shields: shields appear on floor 1.
  • Fast Reinforcements: arrive on the next turn.
  • Glass Heart: start with 2 hearts.

Unlocks after the first boss kill. Each heat level raises tips and unlocks a new boss form. This gives the challenge ceiling you asked for.

07 / SHIFT and the reel

The theme and the showcase stay in the loop

  • Shift positions: the swap is the verb.
  • Night shift: the run is one shift. You clock in at the lobby and clock out at the top or when you die.
  • Shift perspective: the 3D replay sees the room from the swapped spot.
  • Room replay: the 3D POV replay after every cleared room, as it works now.
  • Death reel: the last three swaps before the final heart.
  • Run recap: the best chain kill of the run, with a share code so a friend replays the same tower.

Cut order if time runs short

  1. Ricochet and shotgun.
  2. Barrel.
  3. Heat.
  4. Long Con and Preview Ghost.
  5. Run recap (keep the death reel).

Never cut: budget, hearts, door choice, the death restart.

Risks

  • Solver time: each rule adds search cost. Cap depth at 5 and measure per phase. Fall back to pre-generated room pools.
  • Retry learning: retrying the same room lets you memorize the answer. See the decision below.
  • Frustration: losing at room 11 hurts. Assist mode adds hearts and slack.
  • Scope: this is more than the earlier prototype. The phases keep it testable at each step.

Assist mode (more hearts, more slack, optional no-fail practice) keeps the game finishable for everyone and fits the one-hand, no-timer base.
